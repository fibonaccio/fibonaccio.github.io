My work centers on understanding the evolution of molecular interactions. Using ancestral protein reconstruction, high-throughput functional assays, and quantitative computational frameworks, I investigate the genetic mechanisms that drive the emergence, maintenance, or disruption of molecular interactions. My overarching goal is to identify the genetic determinants of the strength and specificity of interactions among biological molecules.

Ancestral Sequence Reconstruction & Neofunctionalization

By assaying the transcriptional activation phenotypes of thousands of combinatorial variants spanning the sequence space between ancestral nuclear receptors AncSR1 and AncSR2, I am producing the first comprehensive map of how protein function changes across a complete ancestral neofunctionalization. This work characterizes every protein sequence background that permits or prohibits the gain of a new DNA-binding specificity and reveals plausible mutational paths through sequence space.

Genotype–Phenotype Landscapes & Evolutionary Trajectories

By characterizing the full genotype-to-phenotype map of the AncSR1→AncSR2 transition, I identify which substitutions caused the functional shift, quantify each substitution's phenotypic effect, determine the minimum number of mutations required, and reconstruct the specific mutational orders that allow the transition. I develop geometric frameworks for quantifying phenotypic change across sequence space.

Meiotic Drive & Selfish Genetic Elements

During my Ph.D. with Sarah Zanders at the Stowers Institute, I studied wtf killer meiotic drivers in fission yeast (Schizosaccharomyces pombe)—selfish genes that bias their own transmission by destroying spores that did not inherit them. I combined mathematical modeling, experimental evolution, and population genetics to understand how mating systems and multi-driver competition shape driver spread and extinction.

Computational & Quantitative Methods

Across projects I build and apply computational tools—population genetics models, high-throughput sequence-function pipelines, and statistical frameworks—to link molecular variation to evolutionary outcomes. I also teach bioinformatics and R/Bioconductor methods.

2024 G3: Genes | Genomes | Genetics

Modeling the evolution of Schizosaccharomyces pombe populations with multiple killer meiotic drivers

López Hernández JF, Rubinstein BY, Unckless RL, Zanders SE

What happens when a genome carries more than one killer meiotic driver? Using wtf genes as a model, this study shows that duplicate drivers can spread under most conditions, especially when they diverge into distinct variants. Identical duplicates spread more slowly than their parent, while divergent drivers typically both reach fixation. Under specific combinations of recombination and transmission strength, both can be lost. The results explain how natural driver gene families expand and inform synthetic gene drive design.

DOI: 10.1093/g3journal/jkae142

2021 eLife

Diverse mating phenotypes impact the spread of wtf meiotic drivers in Schizosaccharomyces pombe

López Hernández JF, Helston RM, Lange JJ, Eickbush MT, Billmyre RB, Schaffner SH, McCroskey S, Zanders SE

Meiotic drivers depend on outcrossing to spread, yet S. pombe is thought to predominantly self. By measuring mating propensities across diverse natural isolates, this study found that outcrossing rates vary substantially and respond to cell density and partner availability. Mathematical modeling and experimental evolution showed that observed selfing levels slow—but do not prevent—driver spread, explaining how wtf drivers persist despite frequent inbreeding. (Hamilton Award Finalist, SSE 2021)

DOI: 10.7554/eLife.70812

2020 Microbial Cell Factories

Assessment of the ptxD gene as a growth and selective marker in Trichoderma atroviride using Pccg6

Carreras-Villaseñor N, Rico-Ruiz JG, Chávez Montes RA, Yong-Villalobos L, López-Hernández JF, Martínez-Hernández P, Herrera-Estrella L, Herrera-Estrella A, López-Arredondo D

This study evaluated the phosphite dehydrogenase gene ptxD as a selectable marker in the biocontrol fungus Trichoderma atroviride, demonstrating its utility for stable transformation under phosphite selection. The work contributes a new genetic tool for engineering this agriculturally important fungus.

DOI: 10.1186/s12934-020-01326-z

2018 Yeast

Veni, vidi, vici: the success of wtf meiotic drivers in fission yeast

López Hernández JF, Zanders SE

A review examining why wtf genes are among the most successful meiotic drivers known, discussing the poison–antidote mechanism by which they selectively destroy non-carrier spores, their rapid evolutionary diversification, and the ecological and genetic conditions that sustain them in S. pombe populations.

DOI: 10.1002/yea.3305

2016 BMC Genomics

Global transcriptional analysis suggests Lasiodiplodia theobromae pathogenicity factors in modulation of grapevine defensive response

Paolinelli-Alfonso M, Villalobos-Escobedo JM, Rolshausen P, Herrera-Estrella A, Galindo-Sánchez C, López-Hernández JF, Hernandez-Martinez R

RNA-seq-based transcriptome profiling of the interaction between the fungal pathogen Lasiodiplodia theobromae and grapevine, identifying candidate effector genes and host defense pathways modulated during infection. This work provides a genomic foundation for understanding trunk disease in viticulture.

DOI: 10.1186/s12864-016-2952-3

2014 Journal of Proteomics

Plug and Play Assembly of a Low-Temperature Plasma Ionization Mass Spectrometry Imaging (LTP-MSI) System

Maldonado-Torres M, López-Hernández JF, Jiménez-Sandoval P, Winkler R

Description and validation of a low-cost, modular mass spectrometry imaging system based on low-temperature plasma ionization, enabling direct metabolite imaging from biological surfaces without sample preparation. Demonstrated applications in plant and food tissue imaging.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jprot.2014.03.003
